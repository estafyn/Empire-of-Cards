## Society & Roles

### The Family of Four

The fundamental social unit of cassowary society, consisting of four specialized roles that work in harmony:

1. **The Farmer**: Specialized in cultivation, particularly of insect farms and food sources. Stays close to established territories and maintains resources.

2. **The Hunter**: The resource gatherer and protector who ventures beyond established territories to find food and defend against threats.

3. **The Child Rearer**: Responsible for raising and educating young cassowaries, passing down cultural knowledge and survival skills.

4. **The Roamer/Connector**: Often female, this role connects different cassowary groups, shares knowledge, and facilitates courtship and social exchange between families.

## Locations

### Sahul

The ancient landmass comprising modern Australia, Tasmania, and New Guinea when they were connected by land bridges during periods of lower sea levels.

### Daintree Rainforest

One of the oldest continuously surviving rainforests, located in northern Australia. A cradle of cassowary civilization.

### Kakadu

A region in northern Australia's tropical monsoon climate that supported early cassowary settlements and technological development.

### Arnhem Land

A historical region in Australia's Northern Territory, significant in cassowary cultural development.

### Cape York

The northernmost peninsula of Australia, a key connection point between Australian and New Guinean cassowary populations.

### Werribee

A wetland region that developed into a major trade hub, characterized by extensive agriculture and aquaculture operations.

## Historical Eras

### The Cassowary-Emu Divergence (~25,000,000 BCE)

The evolutionary split between cassowaries and emus, with cassowaries specializing in rainforest environments while emus adapted to more open woodlands.

### The Arms Race: Forelimb Evolution (~12,000,000 - 6,000,000 BCE)

Period of rapid evolutionary development of cassowary forelimbs for tool use and manipulation, driven by predator threats and changing environments.

### The Age of Insect Farming (~2,000,000 BCE)

Era when cassowaries developed sophisticated techniques to cultivate insects as a food source, leading to the first stable social units.

### The Age of Fire (~6,000,000 - 1,000,000 BCE)

Transformative period when cassowaries mastered fire use, revolutionizing their diet, defense, and social structures.

### The Age of Expansion (~270,000 BCE)

Period of population growth and territorial spread, with larger settlements forming and inter-group diplomacy developing.

### The Age of Language (~15,000 BCE)

Development of sophisticated vocal communication, enabling complex storytelling, knowledge sharing, and diplomatic negotiations.

### The Age of Written Language (~10,000 BCE)

The emergence of symbolic writing systems, allowing knowledge preservation across generations.

### The Age of Metallurgy (~5,000 BCE)

Period when cassowaries began mining and refining metals, particularly iron ore, to create tools and ceremonial objects.

### The Age of Queens (~2,000 BCE)

Early period of cassowary governance where powerful female leaders known as "Flame Bearers" shaped destiny across Sahul, serving as both political rulers and symbolic keepers of their hearths.

### The Age of Companies (~140,000 BCE)

Industrial-era equivalent in cassowary development, characterized by corporate power structures, standardized trade systems, and the rise of debt-based labor.

## Cultural Concepts

### Courtship Games

Ritualized competitions and activities that test compatibility between potential cassowary mates.

### Worry Stones

Small, smoothed rocks with repetitive handling patterns found near nests, used in anxiety-regulation rituals.

### Hearth Incubation

The practice of nesting eggs near controlled fires, which increased survival rates and supported the development of larger brains.

### The Great Inferno

A catastrophic fire event that shaped cassowary history, marking the transition from innocence to ambition in Sahul's civilizations.

### Ritual Fire Diplomacy

Political negotiations involving elaborate fire-lighting ceremonies and symbolic exchanges of embers, especially prevalent during the Age of Queens.

### Flame Bearers

A title for queens during the Age of Queens, signifying their role as both political rulers and symbolic keepers of their community's sacred hearths.

## Domesticated Species

### Ravens

Highly intelligent birds used as record-keepers and scribes, responsible for documenting events and legal transactions. They can inscribe symbols but rarely express personal opinions.

### Cockatoos

Birds with exceptional mimicry abilities used as messengers, similar to mobile voice notes. Their strong memory and independent personalities make them essential for diplomacy and emergency alerts.

### Eagles

Birds of prey serving as hunting companions and scouts. Their sharp eyesight and loyalty make them valuable for hunting, reconnaissance, and defense.

### Diprotodons

Megafauna used for transportation and powering mechanical infrastructure such as rail systems and ships. They are intelligent and emotionally perceptive, requiring consistent care.

## Economic Systems

### Partially Owned Citizens

Individuals bound by debt contracts who retain some rights but must fulfill labor quotas. This system emerged during the Age of Companies.

### Werribee Traders Association (WTA)

A powerful corporate entity that dominated political and economic life during the Age of Companies, controlling trade routes and resource extraction.

### Corporate Clans

Massive trading companies that formed during the Age of Companies, rivaling governments in influence and power. They organized territories through contracts, debt systems, and ownership stakes.

## Biological Adaptations

### Casque

The distinctive helmet-like structure on a cassowary's head. Evolved to enhance sound reception and, in later eras, became a marker of lineage and status.

### Epigenetic Transmission

The passing of behavioral traits through DNA regulatory changes, particularly associated with anxiety responses and threat assessment abilities.
